# How the games are played

This is what the host and players do in each game. Setup and running are in the [README](../README.md) and
[ONLINE.md](ONLINE.md).

## Jeopardy

**Becoming the host.** The TV's lobby has a small **Hosting? Scan to host** code. Scanning it opens the host page already
signed in (the PIN is built into the code and removed from the address bar at once), so there is nothing to type and no menu
to open. Anyone who scans it becomes a host, the same as typing the PIN. The PIN is also in the Play/Pause menu.

**Fixing your name or colour.** In every game, tap your name at the top of your phone (it has a small pencil) to fix a typo in
your name or pick another colour (in team games only the name changes: the team owns the colour). A name someone else already
has is refused. It changes for everyone straight away.

Jeopardy is played by **individuals, up to ten**, with every phone as a buzzer. It runs on the Apple TV, and the
Siri Remote can do everything the players can (pick a clue, start, continue), so a game can start with nobody
touching the host page. The first player to join is the **captain**, unless someone is hosting: with a host page
connected there is **no captain** (in Jeopardy and in Family Feud alike), and the topics are the host's to choose on the host page. The TV lobby
then shows only who has joined. With **A host judges out loud** on and no host page, the captain is the host: their
phone gets **Open buzzers**, **Correct** and **Wrong** (and can peek at the answer), and taps the clue the player in control
says.

1. **Lobby.** Players scan the QR code (or type the room code) and join. The TV (**Choose questions**), the captain's
   phone or the host page chooses what to play, on three shelves:
   - **Real show**: topics from a random season of the public clue archive, fetched for each room. Pick the era (any,
     classic 80s and 90s, 2000s, recent) and switch topics on or off.
   - **Family packs**: the packs that come with the game (Easy Breezy is the casual, family-friendly one).
   - **Your games**: whole boards written in the question editor (the host page's **Questions** tab), played **just
     as written** (your categories, your values, your Daily Doubles). Pick one, or add a second one as round 2.
     Making one: six categories of five clues and a Final, and it appears on this shelf.
   - Switch on as many topics and packs as you like; the game mixes twelve categories from them. **Preview and swap**
     shows the twelve and lets you swap any of them before you start.
   - **No repeats.** The TV remembers every clue it has shown (on the Apple TV and in the browser, kept between rooms).
     Each topic says how many of its categories are still new, and the game deals new categories first, so a clue only
     comes back once every clue in its pack has been played. Then that pack starts over. **Start fresh** forgets it all.
   - **A host judges out loud** switches typed answers off: players say their answer, and the host (on the host page,
     on the captain's phone when there is no host page, or with the remote) taps Correct or Wrong. **With a host page
     connected this is always on**: phones never show an answer box (or Daily Double bet buttons: the player tells the
     host the bet and the host types it in). Only Final Jeopardy is typed on the phones.
2. **Board.** The player in control picks a clue on their phone (or on the TV with the remote): the phone lists each
   category with its values, they tap a value (it glows on the TV) and then **Open**. With a host (a host page, or host
   judging on), nobody picks on their phone: the player says a category and a value out loud and the host taps it.
   **Category notes:** every category can carry a one-line note on what it asks for ("Every answer is two words that
   rhyme"), shown under its name in the picker and under the clue, so the host can read it out when a name is unclear.
   The shipped sets have one for every category, real-show categories use the show's own explanation when it gave one
   (and a few well-known kinds, like Before & After, are explained), and the question editor has a "Note for the host"
   field. **Sights & Sounds** (picture and sound clues) is on by default beside the real show, and every round
   gets one of its categories. (Your own boards can still carry a video link.) Round 2 is worth
   double and has two Daily Doubles; the lowest score picks first.
3. **Buzzers.** With a host judging, the buzzers never open by themselves: the host reads the clue for as long as
   they like (no clock on the clue or on a Daily Double bet) and taps **Open buzzers**. Without a host, the clue is
   shown and read, then the buzzers open with a **7-second countdown** on the TV and the
   phone. Every phone turns gold at the same instant as the TV, and **the fastest thumb wins, not the fastest Wi-Fi**
   (see Fair buzzing below). A press before the buzzers open locks that player out for a quarter second (the buzzer screen warns about it while you wait). Everyone
   who pressed is timed, and the times are shown after the clue.
4. **Answer.** The winner has **15 seconds** to **type** the answer on their phone. The game checks it, forgiving case,
   articles ("the"), punctuation and small typos, and any "also accept" words on your own clues. **Right** scores and
   gives that player control, and the game goes straight back to the board, as on the show (no answer screen; the score
   count-up carries on in the podiums along the bottom). With a host, the answer is said out loud and the host taps
   Correct or Wrong. **Wrong** (or no answer) deducts the value and opens the buzzers again, for 6 seconds,
   for everyone who has not tried.
5. **Daily Doubles.** The clue stays hidden until the player in control wagers on their phone (with a host, they tell the
   host, who types it in). The wager is from **$0** up to their
   score or the round's top value, whichever is higher: quick buttons for $0, half and all in, and a box to type any amount.
6. **Final Jeopardy.** Players with a positive score wager. **The clue is never shown by the last wager coming in:** when
   everyone is locked in the host (or the captain, or the TV remote's **Show the clue** button) starts it. (If the 60-second
   wager clock runs out first, whoever has not bet bets nothing, and it still waits to be started.) Then everyone types an
   answer in 35 seconds. The answers are shown one at a time on the TV, lowest score first.
7. **Winner.** The champion, everyone's scores, and awards (Fastest Finger, Big Brain, On Fire, High Roller, Fearless).
   **Play again** deals new categories.

**Buzz times.** Everyone's buzz times, ranked and to the thousandth of a second, stay in a semi-transparent panel down the side of the TV (one player to a row, a long name wraps inside its row) for the whole
clue (also after a wrong answer, when the buzzers open again), and for ten seconds on the board after it ends.

Also: streaks (**on fire** after three right in a row), emoji reactions that float up the TV from phones, and
**captain controls** on the captain's phone: choose packs, start, skip a clue, flip the last ruling, continue, end the
round, fix any score, remove a player (before the game), play again, or hand the captain to someone else. If the
captain stays away, someone else takes over. **Changing the captain:** on the Apple TV, select a player's seat in the
lobby, or open the game menu (Play/Pause) at any time and pick **Change the captain**; on the website's TV, click a
seat in the lobby; on the host page, **Make captain** in the Players tab.

**What's going on:** a strip along the bottom of the TV says whose moment it is and what happens next (whose pick and
why, who buzzed, who has to press Continue). On the Apple TV the remote always lands on the one button that moves the
game along (Start game, Show the board, Next, Start round 2, Correct and Wrong for a host, Play again), or on a clue
on the board. Everything else (skip a clue, flip a ruling, the captain, end the round or the game, the host PIN) is
in the game menu: **Play/Pause**, or **Menu** at the end of the bar.

**End game** (on the TV's buttons, the Apple TV's Play/Pause menu, the captain's phone and the host page) stops a game
part way: scores reset and the TV and every phone go back to the lobby, with the players still in. **New room code**
(in the lobby, and in the TV's menu) starts an empty room: nobody from the old room comes along.

If a clue is judged wrongly, **Flip the ruling** reverses it, including the score.


## The host page

The host page (`/host`; for Jeopardy it is optional, since the TV and the captain can run the game) is laid out like an app: **Game**, **Players**, **Scores**, **Settings** and
**Questions** (the question editor) tabs (a bar along the bottom on a phone, a rail down the side on a tablet or
laptop). The one thing to do next is always a large button pinned at the bottom
(Start game, Open buzzers, Correct / Wrong, Back to the board...). Choices open as
slide-up sheets. Game rules and timers live under Settings, and ending the game
is tucked away at the bottom of Settings behind a confirmation.


## Family Feud

Pick **Family Feud** in the game picker. It is always **two teams** (Red and Blue); players are
dealt onto them at random and the host can move anyone.

1. **Setup.** Choose a question set, the winning score (200 to 500) and whether to play Fast Money.
2. **Face-off.** One player from each team steps up (a different player each round; the host can
   change who). The host reads the question and opens the buzzers; only those two phones can buzz.
   The first one answers out loud and the host taps the matching survey answer, or **Not on the board**.
   The top answer wins the face-off outright; otherwise the other player gets a go and the better answer
   wins. **If both miss**, the next players in line answer: the team that buzzed first leads off with its
   next player, then the other team's next player, and so on down both lines. The first answer on the board
   wins the face-off. Only if everyone on both teams misses does the face-off start again.
3. **Play or pass.** The winners choose (anyone on that team can tap **Play** or **Pass** on their phone). Their team then names answers; the host taps each one and it
   flips on the TV. A **strike** puts a big boxed X in the middle of the TV, which then slides down into
   the next empty strike box. Three **strikes** and the other team gets one **steal** guess: a good steal takes the
   whole pot, a miss leaves it with the first team, and the big X comes up on the TV for it too (the host's button
   says "Wrong: show the X").
   **Who is up.** The host page (and the TV's banner, and that player's phone) says which player on the team is up: the
   players take turns, starting with the one after the team's face-off player, and it moves on after every guess.
   **Answer clock.** When a player is up the host taps **Start the 10s clock** (the length is under Settings → Rules).
   The seconds count down on the TV and the host page, and it says TIME'S UP at zero; it never strikes by itself: the
   host decides. A guess stops it.
   **Buzz times.** In the face-off the TV and the host page show who buzzed and how fast, to the thousandth of a second.
4. **Scoring.** Points on the board are banked when the round ends. Rounds 4 and 5 are worth double and
   triple (switch off under Settings). First team to the winning score, or the last round, ends the
   rounds. **The answers nobody guessed** are turned over by the host one at a time, as on the show: after the round, tap an
   answer on the host page (or **Turn the next answer**; the captain's phone has the same button) and it flips on the TV. Nothing is scored.
   **Sudden death.** If the last round ends and nobody has reached the winning score, one more
   face-off is played (a new question) and the winning answer is worth **five times** its points, or **ten times**
   when five is not enough to get there; a team that still hasn't made it after that plays another, until
   somebody does. Switch it off under Settings → Rules and the game ends after the last round as before.
   **The team ahead then has won the game**, and the TV crowns them before anything else.
   Fast Money is their **bonus round**, played for the jackpot: it cannot change who won (the host or
   the captain can also skip it). Only a **tie** leaves the winner open, and then Fast Money is the
   **tiebreaker**: the host picks the team that plays, reaching the goal wins them the game, and falling
   short gives it to the other team. With Fast Money switched off a tie stays a tie.
**No host?** Switch on **No host: type answers on phones** (the first player to join, the captain, sees it on
their phone; it is also on the host page). Then the buzzers open by themselves, whoever's turn it is types the
guess, and the survey checks it (each answer's aliases count too). A guess that is already on the board is not a
strike. The captain's phone starts the game, picks the question set and moves on to the next round; Fast Money
moves on when time is up and reveals itself. Every phone shows the question, big; the answer board stays on the TV
(phones never show it).

5. **Fast Money.** The winning team (or, after a tie, the team the host picks) chooses two players (the host taps who is Player 1 and Player 2 on the host page, can swap them, and can change whoever is up while they answer; the same player cannot be both). It goes as on the show:
   1. **Player 1 plays.** Player 2 waits out of the room (or with headphones on). Player 1 answers five questions
      (40 seconds by default; change it under Settings): typing on their phone, or the host enters what they say.
   2. **Player 1's answers are revealed.** One answer at a time: the host taps **Reveal the next answer** (the TV types
      out what they said), then again for the points ("survey says"). Player 1's total stays on the TV.
   3. **They are hidden, and Player 2 comes in.** The host taps **Hide the answers. Player 2 is next**. The TV covers
      Player 1's answers but keeps their score on screen, and Player 2 plays (45 seconds by default).
   4. **Player 2's answers are revealed** the same way, with Player 1's back in view, and the TV shows the total against the goal.
   **Player 2 cannot say what Player 1 said.** On a phone such an answer is not accepted (the phone says so and asks for
   another) and when the host enters one it is turned away too, with a red note for that question. The TV sounds the
   buzzer and says which question to try again.
   **Getting the answers in.** On the phone the questions come one at a time with a big answer box and a microphone
   button; **Skip** (or just saying "skip", or sending nothing) moves on, and after the last question it goes back to
   any skipped ones while there is time, then shows a review with a **Send my answers** button. Answers go to the host
   as you go, and whatever is there is sent when time runs out. (The microphone button uses the browser's speech
   recognition. Browsers usually only allow that on a secure `https` page, which the online site is, but a plain-`http`
   server is not, so there it may say "Voice isn't allowed here"; the microphone on the phone's own keyboard always works
   and fills the box the same way.) When the host enters the answers, each question shows the survey answers as
   **one-tap buttons**: tap the one they said, or type something else (Enter moves to the next box). The answers
   Player 1 already used are greyed out for Player 2. Each survey list is shown on the host screen with a tag where each
   player's answer landed, so the rank is visible without opening anything, and the points button already says what it
   will score (for example **#1 Apple · +34**). An answer left blank is a zero that needs just one tap, and shows as
   NO ANSWER. **Reach 200 points and they win right there**: the TV shows the jackpot for a moment and then the winners of the
   Feud, whatever answers are left. The host page still has every Fast Money question, all its survey answers (#1 first) and what
   each player said, so the host can read out what was never turned over. **Mistakes can be put right at any time**, even after
   the game is over: tap **Edit** on any answer to change what was said and its points (the score and the winner follow).
6. **Undo.** A mis-tap is easy on a phone, so **Undo** takes back the last reveal, strike or choice,
   including the points.

Question sets are written in the editor (see [QUESTION-SETS.md](QUESTION-SETS.md)). Each has 3 to 8
rounds of a question with 3 to 8 ranked answers (points are how many out of 100 said it), optional
"also accept" words for matching, and exactly five Fast Money questions. Five sample sets ship with the
repo; their survey numbers are made up, not real survey results.


## Spy Night

A game of secret roles for **five to ten players**, each on their own phone. It is the same kind of game as the
board game *The Resistance* and *Werewolf*, with its own name and look (base game only: no extra modules).

**Roles.** When the game starts every phone shows a secret card. Most players are the **Resistance**; the rest are
**Spies**: 2 Spies with 5 or 6 players, 3 with 7, 8 or 9, and 4 with 10. The Spies' phones list the other Spies; the
Resistance learn nothing. The role card stays hidden until you tap it, and a **My role** button lets you look again.
Neither the TV nor the host page ever shows a role while the game is running, so the host can play too.

**Five missions.** The Resistance win by completing three; the Spies win by failing three.
1. The **leader** (it passes clockwise after every vote) picks a team, which may include themselves. The mission
   sizes are 2/3/2/3/3 with 5 players, 2/3/4/3/4 with 6, 2/3/3/4/4 with 7, and 3/4/4/5/5 with 8, 9 or 10.
2. Everyone votes **Approve** or **Reject** on their phone. Votes show together when the last one is in. More than
   half approving sends the team; a tie rejects. Five rejected teams in a row and the Spies win.
3. The team members each play **Success** or **Fail** in secret. The Resistance can only play Success; a Spy may play
   either. One Fail card fails the mission (two are needed on the fourth mission with 7 or more players). Everyone
   sees how many Fail cards there were, never who played them.

**On the phones.** A Spy's phone shows their role card (tap to see it), and the **other Spies are behind a separate button**
("Show the other Spies"), so a glance at the screen gives nothing away. The mission screen says it plainly: the Resistance **have
to** play Success; a Spy may play Success or Fail. Everyone may talk about anything out loud as much as they like (who they trust,
accusations, bluffing): the only rule is to keep your screen to yourself.

**Who went.** Under each of the five mission boxes (on the TV, the phones and the host page) are the names of the players who
went on that mission. When the game ends, each name also shows whether they played Success (✓) or Fail (✗).

**Music.** A noir lobby tune plays while players join, and a quieter, tenser loop plays under the game from the deal until the
game is over.

**Who starts it.** With a host page open, the host presses **Deal the roles**. Without one, the first player to join
does, from their phone. Anyone who goes away does not hold things up: their vote counts as Reject, and their mission
card as Success. The host (or the first player) can also **Move on without waiting**. Past votes are public, so the
phones keep a "Who voted for what" list to argue over. When the game ends everyone's role is shown.

## Pie Chase

The wedge-collecting trivia board game for **two to six players or teams**, each with a phone. It plays by the classic
rules of the well-known board game, with its own name and look.

**The board.** A wheel: a ring of 42 spaces, six spokes of five spaces, and the **hub** in the middle (73 spaces in
all). On the ring are the six **headquarters** (one per category, where the spokes meet the ring), twelve **Roll Again**
spaces, and category spaces in the six colours. The categories are Geography (blue), Entertainment (pink), History
(yellow), Arts & Literature (purple), Science & Nature (green) and Sports & Leisure (orange); a question set may rename
them, but the colours stay.

**Who goes first.** Everyone starts in the hub and rolls the die on their phone. The highest roll goes first; a tie for
highest rolls again between the tied players. Play then passes to the left (the order players joined).

**A turn.**
1. Roll the die on your phone, then move **exactly** that many spaces, in any direction. You may not double back on a
   move, but you may turn at a headquarters or the hub, and you may cross the hub. When the roll can end in more than
   one place, your phone (and the TV) number the choices, and you tap one.
2. Where you land decides what happens:
   - a **category space**: a question in that colour;
   - a **headquarters**: a question in its category, and a right answer wins that **wedge** (if you do not have it yet);
   - **Roll Again**: roll again, no question;
   - the **hub** (before you have every wedge): you pick any category for your question.
3. **Right**: you roll again. **Wrong**: play passes to the left.

**Who asks.** The question shows on the TV and the answering player's phone, **never the answer**. The next player (on
the answerer's left) is the **reader**: their phone shows the question and answer, they read it out, listen, and tap
**Right** or **Wrong**. With a host page open, the host reads and judges instead, and the phones cannot. The answer
shows on the TV once it has been judged.

**Winning.** Once you have every wedge, head for the hub; you must land on it **by exact count**. The other players
then pick the category for your winning question (on their phones, or the host taps it). Answer it right and you win.
Answer it wrong and your turn ends: leave the hub on your next turn and come back to try again.

**Options** (host page, Settings, before the game starts):
- **Wedges to win**: 6 (classic), 5 or 4 for a shorter game. With fewer than six you still need a wedge from that many
  different headquarters.
- **Equal turns**: when someone wins, the rest of that round still plays, so everyone has had the same number of turns.
  Anyone else who wins in that round ties.

**Teams.** In teams mode each team shares one token and pie, and any member can roll, move and answer.

**Away players.** A player whose phone goes quiet does not hold the game up: the host (or, with no host, the first player)
can **Skip** their turn from **Someone gone quiet?**, and the reader passes to the next player who is here.

**Questions.** Two sets ship. *Family Easy* (360 questions, the default) is everyday trivia that kids and grown-ups can
all answer; *Family Classic* (362) is harder, closer to the real board game's level. The host picks one in Settings
before the game starts, or "All question sets (mixed)" to play them together. None are copied from the real game's
cards. Write your own in the **Questions** tab: six categories, as many questions as you like in each, or paste a list of
`question | answer` lines. A TV does not ask the same question twice until a category runs out.

**Sounds.** Pie Chase borrows Jeopardy's built-in recordings: a roll of the die, a question coming up, right and wrong,
a wedge won, the winning question, and applause at the end. Your own Jeopardy sound files are used here too, and files
uploaded for Pie Chase itself take their place.

## Guys vs Gals

The battle of the sexes: **the Guys against the Gals**, each player on their own phone. The Guys answer questions about
gal stuff, and the Gals answer questions about guy stuff. Who knows the other side better?

**Sides.** Everyone picks **I'm a Guy** or **I'm a Gal** on their phone in the lobby (the host can move anyone). Both
sides need at least one player. The first player to join (or the host) starts the battle.

**The path.** A row of coloured spaces runs across the TV: the Guys' winner's circle and green space on the left,
START in the middle, and the Gals' green space and winner's circle on the right. One trophy starts on START. Going out
from START each way, the spaces are Our Facts (START itself), Our Stuff, Our Pics, Our Lives, Our Moves, and green.
**The Gals go first.**

**A turn.** A player on the *other* team reads a card from their own side's deck (the Guys read guy stuff to the
Gals), and readers take turns. The space the trophy is on picks the deck:

- **Our Facts** and **Our Stuff**: the reader's phone shows the question and the answer. The team says its answer out
  loud and the reader taps **Right** or **Wrong**. Only the reader's phone shows the answer, like holding the card.
- **Our Pics**: the same, with a picture on the TV.
- **Our Lives**: a question about the reader, with three answers. The reader secretly taps the one that's true for them,
  then the team guesses on their phones (the team's favourite counts, a tie goes to the one picked first).
- **Our Moves**: charades. The reader picks someone on the answering team, both see the same thing to act out, and both
  act it at the same time while each team shouts at its own actor. The reader taps who said it first (one minute).
- **Green**: any deck but Our Moves.

**Right** pulls the trophy one space toward the answering team, and they get another card. **Wrong** hands the turn to
the other team, with the trophy where it is (the spaces count the same on either side of START). In a charade, if the
reader's team shouts it first, the trophy moves their way and it is their turn.

**Trophies.** A right answer while the trophy is on your own green space wins it: it goes into your winner's circle, a
new trophy goes on START, and the other team plays first. **The first team to three trophies wins** (the host can
make it one or two).

**Challenges.** Each team has three challenge tokens. On your turn (not on an Our Moves card) tap **Challenge** to throw
the card back: the other team has to answer it themselves, without the reader's help (on Our Lives, the reader's
teammates guess), and the reader still judges. Nobody on their team has seen the answer, because only the reader's
phone shows it. If they miss, the trophy comes your way and you keep playing; if they get it,
the trophy goes their way and it is their turn.

**The race.** Pick **Race** instead of **Tug of war** in the lobby (on the first player's phone, or the host page) for
the classic pawn race. A coin toss says who goes first. Each team has two pawns (one in a quick race, set on the host
page): one starts on its own START, the other waits. A reader from the other team reads a card of three questions, and
every right answer moves the team's pawn one space and brings the next question; a miss, or all three right, ends the
turn. Land on your own side's wild space and you draw a wild card at once; end your turn on the other side's wild space
and you draw one of theirs (wild cards help the side they belong to: move ahead, send the other team back, make them
sit out a turn). Once a game, a team that ends its turn on the other team's pawn takes it prisoner, while the other
team still has a pawn waiting; the prisoner is freed back to START when their other pawn gets across. The first team
with every pawn across wins. There are no challenges in the race.

**Reactions.** Once the game is under way, the phones have a row of emoji. They float up the sender's side of the TV.

**The host.** The host page shows every card with its answer and can judge it (as well as the reader), pick the actor,
call a charade, lock in Our Lives guesses, throw out a dud with **New card**, nudge the trophy a space either way, and
end the game. Before the game: who is on which side, the question set, trophies to win (1, 2 or 3) and challenge tokens
on or off.

**Questions.** *Family Battle* has all five decks for both sides, all written for this game (none copied from the
board game): over 160 Our Facts and Our Stuff questions per side, more than 25 Our Pics, 32 Our Lives and 44 Our Moves.
Write your own in the **Questions** tab: each half has the five decks, or paste a list (`question | answer`,
`picture | question | answer`, `question | A | B | C`, or one charade per line). Only Our Facts or Our Stuff is a must;
an empty deck is played as Our Facts. A TV does not deal the same card twice until the deck runs out.

**Sounds.** Guys vs Gals has its own recordings, all CC0 (credits beside them): lobby music, a card dealt, a challenge,
the trophy sliding, right and wrong, music under a charade, a trophy won and a fanfare for the winners. Files uploaded
for Guys vs Gals take their place.

## Players and teams

- **Jeopardy** is always individuals, up to ten players.
- **Family Feud** is always two teams, Red and Blue. Players are dealt onto them at random and the host can move anyone,
  rename a team, or **Shuffle teams** in the lobby. Rearranging only happens in the lobby; once a game starts nothing
  moves, and anyone who joins late is put on the smaller team.
- On a phone in Family Feud the **team colour lights up**: a frame round the screen and a banner with the team name and
  teammates.

**Timers (Jeopardy).** All of them are shown on the TV and on the phones.

| Timer | Default |
|---|---|
| Reading the clue | with a host judging, none: the host opens the buzzers; otherwise a few seconds, by the clue's length |
| Buzz in (after the clue is read) | 7 seconds, 6 after a wrong answer |
| Typed answer | 15 seconds; a Daily Double gets 20 after the clue is read |
| Spoken answer, with a host judging | the host's choice: 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 seconds or none (the host then still has 8 seconds to rule) |
| Final Jeopardy answer | 35 seconds |

**Early buzzes.** Pressing before the buzzers open locks that player out for 0.25 seconds, as on the show. The phone
shows it the instant you press (the button flashes red and buzzes in your hand), and the TV keeps the same lockout.

**Fair buzzing.** Each phone keeps its clock in step with the TV's: every few seconds it asks the TV the time, and
keeps the answer that came back quickest, the way network time works. The TV tells every phone ahead of time when the
buzzers will open, so all the buttons turn gold together, and each press is stamped with when it happened on the TV's
clock. The TV then waits a split second (at most 0.35 seconds, and only as long as the slowest phone's messages take)
for a press that happened earlier but is still on its way, and the earliest press wins. A phone resends its press
until the TV confirms it, so a Wi-Fi hiccup doesn't lose a buzz, and checks its connection the moment it comes back
from the lock screen. The whole middle of the screen is the buzzer, it fires the moment a finger lands, and it buzzes
in your hand (Android, and iPhones on iOS 18 or later).

**Reconnects.** A phone that sleeps or refreshes rejoins as the same player with
its score (an id is kept in the browser). The whole game, scores included, is
saved and survives a restart (inside the app on the Apple TV, or in `data/state.json` in server mode).


## Sound

The TV plays short sound effects: buzzers open, a buzz-in, correct, wrong, a Daily Double, Final
Jeopardy, time's up, and a tick for the last five seconds. Jeopardy uses recorded sounds that came over from the Buzzworthy game, with a theme song ("Happy Dance") looping on the main menu and in every game's lobby and thinking music during Final Jeopardy; they are free to use (CC0, credits in `public/builtin-sounds/jeopardy/CREDITS.txt`). Family Feud has its own original theme tune, right-answer ding and face-off buzzer (made for this project, CC0, credits in `public/builtin-sounds/feud/CREDITS.txt`), and its other sounds are synthesized. Spy Night has its own original sounds and a noir-style lobby tune (made for this project, CC0, credits in `public/builtin-sounds/spynight/CREDITS.txt`): the roles being dealt, a vote starting, a team approved or rejected, a mission succeeding or failing, and a win for each side. Your own files replace any of them. They are built in, so nothing needs installing.
The host can turn sound off, change the volume, or press **Test on TV** at the bottom of the host page.
To use your own sound files, see the Apple TV app's [CUSTOM-FILES.md](../tv-app/docs/CUSTOM-FILES.md) (online mode) or [SERVER-MODE.md](SERVER-MODE.md).
